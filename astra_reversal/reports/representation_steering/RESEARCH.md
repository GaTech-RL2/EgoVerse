# Evidence and proposed next steps

The supplied September 26 note, *Reasoning-Guided Test-Time Policy Learning*, leaves the intervention mechanism open. Its central question is whether a grounded correction first improves assisted execution and can then become autonomous behavior. This report separates that hypothesis from measured results. The complete 367-line note was reviewed; its file digest is retained in the publication manifest.

## What the primary sources support

**Text representations.** The VLA text-latent paper averages text-token states over all frames of 20 demonstrations per source task, preserving layer/token coordinates. TEI blends source input embeddings; TLI adds a changing difference of two source residual tensors. It reports π0 OOD success increasing from 9% to 83%, across 20 compositions and ten runs per task. This supports testing existing skill composition. It does not establish autonomous diagnosis, visual-token editing, or transfer of those numbers to our π0.5 checkpoint and one-reset screen. [Paper, equations 2–4 and §4](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.03500v5).

**Flow reversal and learning.** FRS reverses a coarse reference, then denoises it with finite Euler integration. Its own derivation states that exact integration reconstructs the reference; finite-step behavior is part of the mechanism. The paper also uses the resulting noise/action experience for small noise policies trained by BC or RL. It motivates matched direct-reference and native controls, rather than assuming reversal must improve every reference. [Paper, §4 and Appendix E](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.13675v2).

**Local correction and retention.** FlowCorrect adds a small LoRA correction to a frozen ManiFlow field, then trains an observation-conditioned gate. It uses successful uncorrected anchor rollouts; its ablations find worse ID retention without the gate or anchors. Corrections come from humans and use a different point-cloud/absolute-pose interface. Adapting this recipe to our VLA and Astra proposals is a new experiment, not a reproduced result. [Paper, §IV–V](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.22056v1).

**Reflective test-time planning.** This method samples candidate actions, scores them before execution, and reassesses decisions with hindsight. It updates both an internal reflection model and an action LLM; retrospective scalar scores train the latter with REINFORCE. Thus reflection plus parameter updates is prior art, but this is not evidence for our binary promotion gate or continuous-action noise BC. [Paper, §3 and Appendix P](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.21198v2).

**Failure explanation.** REFLECT organizes multisensory experience into sensory, event and subgoal summaries, then uses failure explanations to guide a corrective planner. Its contribution motivates showing the first lost subgoal and relevant temporal evidence instead of asking for a broad diagnosis from a final image. It does not demonstrate our proposed VLA representation intervention or autonomous distillation. [Published paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v229/liu23g.html), [authors' implementation](https://github.com/real-stanford/reflect).

## Practical hypotheses, ordered by the available evidence

1. **Test a stronger intervention before expanding training.** The completed FRS development had 18 tie judgments and no admitted updates. The optimizer therefore has no measured responsibility for that study's lack of learned improvement. The new screen tests text and visual representations directly, with matched native retries and random interventions. A useful milestone is a verified assisted rescue, followed by a separate Astra-disabled learning test.
2. **Keep donor semantics explicit.** TEI selects between two source embeddings; TLI uses a signed contrast and has a neutral point at α=0.5. A “lift higher” behavior must be demonstrated, not inferred from a task name. Phase-triggered source choices are plausible when a clock switches too early; compare them with the published schedule before attributing a gain to reasoning.
3. **Separate pixels, visual embeddings and visual residuals.** Pixel blends alter the encoder's input. VEI changes projected visual tokens. VLI changes visual slots after decoder blocks. They may change action predictions differently even for the same donor. Use the same donor, noise, reset and coefficient where comparing them; native parity at zero and a nonzero action effect are implementation checks, not success evidence.
4. **Localize only after global edits show a failure mode.** Blending a whole donor scene can transplant an unrelated gripper or object arrangement. A later region-mask experiment could retain the current camera's unselected patches and modify only visually justified object/goal regions. Patch alignment, occlusion and mask validity require explicit tests. The current protocol uses full paired camera grids; it does not claim this localized variant.
5. **Learn the correction without keeping the assistance.** If assisted data becomes useful, compare native-objective imitation of executed, correctly anchored action windows with small noise-policy BC. Preserve earlier successful behavior as an anchor and evaluate with Astra and correction memory off. Do not train on an unexecuted chunk suffix or assume averaging different successful noise modes preserves them.
6. **Audit the judge before treating ties as a learning law.** Keep task success separate from visible partial progress; allow uncertainty, mask method identities, and test presentation-order consistency on completed pairs. Training on clearly useful segments or success-filtered imitation is a separate candidate if whole-rollout promotion remains uninformative. These are proposed changes, not changes to the ongoing frozen FRS evaluation.

The prior TLI and TLI-plus-annotation arms both ended at 13/20; that does not test or disprove true visual-token steering. The pixel study's 12/20 uses a different seed and baseline. No pooled causal effect, 100% reliability, novelty claim, or superiority over a matched RL baseline follows from these measurements.
