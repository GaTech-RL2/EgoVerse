# Astra flow reversal and policy improvement

This study answers the expanded request of September 25, 2026. The released
LIBERO-OOD benchmark has **20 tasks**, not a larger unseen inventory. Our first
baseline covered all 20 with ten trials each (86/200); subsequent intervention
studies covered all 20 with one reset each. Task coverage and repeat-trial
coverage are different. The current pixel-perturbation study continues unchanged.

## Frozen experiment plan

The machine-readable protocol is
[frs_policy_improvement_v1.json](configs/frs_policy_improvement_v1.json).
Use the pinned OOD environment and frozen pi05 checkpoint already verified by
the project. Each OOD task gets one adaptation reset (index 0) and ten separately
captured evaluation resets (indexes 1–10), seed 43. These are familiar task
compositions with different reset states, not held-out task types. Evaluation
outcomes never select prompts, rules, training examples, or checkpoints.

Run the same three inspected seed-19 development tasks first to verify wiring.
Every method has 300 simulator actions and executes ten actions per policy query.
Both integration directions use Euler with ten steps. The environment applies
ten stabilization actions outside that budget. All model, simulation and actual
training computation runs on allocated OSMO L40S GPUs.

The final comparison includes six methods on all 200 evaluation episodes:

1. Native pi05, independently sampled full Gaussian noise.
2. Native pi05, one fresh seven-dimensional Gaussian vector repeated across the
   action chunk, plus independent Gaussian padding. This controls the temporal
   noise correlation used by the learned noise policy.
3. Astra directional guidance executed directly, with native policy deferral.
4. Astra directional guidance passed through flow reversal, then generated by
   pi05, with native policy deferral.
5. Rollout critique and prediction editing through reversal, without learning.
   Three adaptation rounds select rules by Astra's judgment; the final rules
   remain fixed on evaluation resets while Astra chooses online action edits.
6. A learned noise policy, evaluated without Astra after each of three adaptation
   rounds. The final checkpoint is determined by the fixed round count, not
   evaluation success. Repeated unchanged checkpoints are labeled explicitly.

## Paper-like directional steering

The reference is [Flow Reversal Steering, v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13675v2),
especially Sections 4.1–4.3 and Appendices D–E. Astra replaces its VLM. At each
ten-action boundary it sees the external camera, task, and a calibrated gripper
plumb line. It chooses a direction and magnitude, or defers for fine manipulation.
The line is only a navigation aid for Astra; both policy cameras remain raw.

The unit direction becomes the normalized translation channels of a ten-action
reference. Camera directions are first mapped to controller signs using the
actual calibration and displayed-image orientation; image-right must not be
assumed to mean positive world Y. Small motions halve it. Unnormalized rotation and gripper values are
zero. Checkpoint transforms supply the exact remaining normalization and padding.
Reverse this reference, replace only padding noise with fresh Gaussian samples,
then generate under the same raw observation and original instruction. The
direct-control arm executes the decoded reference instead of reversing it.

This implementation uses the checkpoint's time convention: action at t=0,
noise at t=1. Reversal runs 0→1 and generation 1→0. Finite-step displacement is
measured, not rejected by an identity-reconstruction tolerance. An exact ODE
round trip would reproduce the reference; feasibility or refinement is an
empirical outcome. Our earlier high-accuracy inversion experiments addressed a
different mechanism and cannot establish this paper's result.

## Critique, edit, reverse, learn

Each task and adaptation arm begins with the same repeated-noise baseline
rollout. Then repeat the following **three times**, including after success:

1. Astra reviews up to four recorded paired-camera snapshots and executed
   controller actions from the latest same-arm rollout. It writes at most five
   short rules with observable triggers and corrective actions.
2. Restore the exact initial scene. Every ten actions, generate the current
   native prediction and show it to Astra with both current cameras, robot state,
   rules, and the last two decisions. Astra may defer or adjust translation by
   at most 0.5 controller units per axis for 1–10 steps and optionally open/close
   the gripper. Rotations are preserved. Any resulting bound clipping is recorded.
3. Normalize the edited prediction and invert it through the frozen flow model.
   Average its seven control-noise channels over the chunk and repeat them;
   padding is independently Gaussian. Generate the executed action chunk from
   this noise under the current raw observation and original task language.
4. Astra compares the candidate rollout with the best previously judged rollout,
   using images and executed actions. Only a `better` verdict promotes it.
   Simulator success, object poses and dense rewards are excluded from these
   requests. A shorter trajectory may reveal termination indirectly.
5. In the learning arm, train a task-specific small visual noise actor on all
   accepted rollout noise labels. The actor predicts seven repeated noise
   channels; pi05 remains the frozen action decoder. Use 1,000 fixed Adam steps
   per accepted round, MSE plus a small output regularizer. In the no-learning
   arm, retain promoted rules without updating weights.

An untrained actor exactly uses the repeated-noise baseline. Rejected or failed
online Astra calls defer to the fresh current policy prediction; old edits never
persist, and no unlogged retry is allowed. Critique/judge failures do not promote
a rollout or update weights. All costs include unsuccessful and rejected calls.

## Evidence and reporting

Before evaluation freeze source, payload, prompts, protocol, reset and weight
hashes. Verify native sampler parity; preserve raw/reference/normalized actions,
pre/post-padding and executed noise, raw observations, decisions, flow counts,
executed actions and checkpoints. Audit transformations, decision timing,
pairing, training labels and provider usage independently of task success.

The report will include exact prompts and request schemas, all 20 task results,
direct versus reversed guidance, critiques/rules and actual action changes,
success by adaptation round, held-out-reset success of each checkpoint, judge
agreement with simulator outcomes, and all input/output/reasoning token usage.
Failures at the cap remain censored rather than assigned an invented iteration
count. Separate adaptation, development, evaluation and rejected-call costs.

The [OOD paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03500v5) evaluates ten runs per task;
our reset/noise scheme and pi05 checkpoint differ from its pi0 experiment.
The FRS paper's original evaluation uses different tasks and fifty trials per
task. This is a method adaptation on LIBERO-OOD, not a reproduction of its
numerical table. Without a matched RL baseline, elapsed time and token counts
cannot establish greater efficiency than value-based RL.
