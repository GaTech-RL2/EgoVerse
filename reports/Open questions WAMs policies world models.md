# Failure coverage, not model size, decides dexterous planning

The comparison between World Action Models (WAMs), plain policies and action-conditioned world models has stopped being about who wins on a benchmark. It is now about **when a learned model of the future is trustworthy enough to make decisions it was never trained on**. A few things are close to settled. Visual fidelity is a poor proxy for control utility. Most of a WAM's benefit comes from video supervision at training time, not from imagining futures at test time. World models trained mostly on successes are optimistic about failure-inducing actions. What stays open is exactly the regime your RSS 2027 paper targets. Nobody has shown a learned visual or latent world model optimizing a genuinely new objective class (avoidance, constraints, risk) zero-shot. Nobody has isolated stochastic from deterministic prediction for manipulation. Nobody has measured whether dexterous world models predict drops and slips at all: the 2025–2026 hand world models are scored on FVD and keypoint accuracy. Your hypothesis sits in that gap. Its failure-data half overlaps heavily with "World Models Dream of Success" (arXiv 2610.09134), so the contribution has to be the dexterous, planning-time, risk-calibration framing. The ranked shortlist below puts (1) a data-coverage × model-expressiveness factorial with calibration as the metric first, then (2) where hand futures are actually multimodal, (3) what limits planning on new objectives, (4) a minimal matched policy vs world-model vs WAM comparison, and (5) whether egocentric human data helps or hurts calibration. One caveat applies throughout. **The underlying research could not fetch arxiv.org full texts.** Every finding comes from search-result summaries, abstracts and secondary reviews. All numbers should be checked against the papers before you cite them, and many 2026 arXiv IDs (26xx.xxxxx) were never verified at source.

## What the field treats as settled, and what is still contested

The three model families are converging. WAM surveys now define a WAM as a model whose predicted future directly helps produce, score, verify or train the action ([WAM survey 2606.20781](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.20781)). They list the same open problems: action alignment, world-action factorization, multi-view consistency, long-horizon memory, neural simulation, efficient inference and safe deployment. They also warn that **generation fidelity does not show action grounding or control utility** ([survey 2609.16074](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.16074v1)). A decision-centric position paper goes further. It proposes an L0–L7 ladder from visual plausibility up to policy-optimization utility, treats interventional testing (L4) as the minimum bar for calling something a decision world model, and observes that **fixed-policy ranking agreement appears in only about a dozen works** ([arXiv 2606.15032](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15032)). Functional benchmarks back the warning: WorldArena's perceptual index correlates with downstream planning success at only **r = 0.36**, as reported by a citing paper ([WorldArena](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08971); [Motubrain](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.27792)).

On the WAM-versus-policy axis, controlled within-paper ablations usually find that video prediction helps. The gains are large on CALVIN-style benchmarks (GR-1, Seer, VPP) and small on saturated ones: Cosmos Policy loses **1.5 points** without its auxiliary future/value objectives and **3.9 points** without video pretraining ([GR-1](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/2c37c5bcef24b9541550261dcd63261b-Paper-Conference.pdf); [Cosmos Policy](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/cosmos-lab/cosmos-policy/)). Cross-paper headlines such as DreamZero's 39.5% vs 16.3% task progress on unseen tasks mix up backbone, corpus and scale ([DreamZero](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.15922v1)). The best robustness study finds WAMs more robust to visual distractors. But **π0.5 matches them (85.7% on LIBERO-Plus) with diverse data and no explicit world modelling**, and camera-viewpoint and initial-state shifts still hurt WAMs ([arXiv 2603.22078](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22078)). Fast-WAM keeps video co-training but skips test-time imagination, and stays competitive at about 4× lower latency ([survey 2606.20781](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.20781)). BadWAM independently reports the same training-time benefit ([BadWAM](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.15207)). A design study finds that generated frames influence actions mainly through temporal structure rather than visual detail, and that auxiliary world-modelling losses help only under some training schedules ([arXiv 2609.24048](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.24048)).

Test-time planning is where the contest is liveliest, and it is the part most relevant to you. "Beyond Visual Quality" picks the sampled WAM candidate with the best realized outcome and finds large oracle headroom. Selectors based on visual quality, physical consistency or task progress recover little of it; a learned value recovers a small amount ([arXiv 2609.24745](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24745)). Against that, action-state consistency does separate successful from failed rollouts, though predictions that collapse to a static background game the score ([arXiv 2605.07514](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.07514)). Cosmos Policy's best-of-N with a value head trained on rollouts adds about **12.5 points** on real ALOHA, but only on the training tasks ([liner review](https://liner.com/review/cosmos-policy-finetuning-video-models-for-visuomotor-control-and-planning)). The planning literature itself is almost entirely goal-image based. V-JEPA 2-AC lists image-only goals, camera sensitivity and long-horizon error as limitations ([V-JEPA 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.09985)). Hierarchical MPPI on latent models gets to 70% real pick-and-place from one goal image ([HWM](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.03208v2)). Constraint- and risk-aware MPPI exists only with explicit dynamics models ([BC-MPPI](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.00272); [COSMIK-MPPI](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.10358)).

## Known failure modes cluster at off-nominal actions and contact outcomes

The failure modes reported across papers share one pattern: **models are right where the data was dense and wrong exactly where a decision is consequential**. In the Veo policy-evaluation study, Pearson correlation is 0.91 for background shifts but **0.56 for novel objects**, and predicted absolute success is consistently too low ([Veo eval](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.10675v1)). Under off-expert action queries, models either ignore the commanded action or produce visually invalid rollouts ([WorldEcho 2608.24885](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.24885v1)). Strong action alignment does not guarantee the correct physical outcome ([WorldOracle](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.18826)). Models predict successful grasps even when the arm is still away from the object ([RLVR-World](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.13934)). One reproduction confirms a model's rankings while finding its absolute scale wrong ([arXiv 2611.02699](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.02699)). Inside WAMs, the named pathologies are a "validity gap" from conflicting video and action supervision ([GE-Act 2.0](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.05588)) and "dream right but act wrong" ([BadWAM](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.15207)).

Model exploitation is a known risk in principle. Surveys warn that optimizing inside an imagined world "may discover artifacts that improve imagined reward without improving real manipulation" ([arXiv 2606.00113](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00113)). Yet **no study was found that measures predicted vs realized return as a function of planning budget** for a visual manipulation world model. Video uncertainty quantification is young. Ensembles are expensive for video models, and calibrated latent probes and hallucination predictors are 2025–26 proposals ([C^3 probe](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05927); [arXiv 2606.27326](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.27326)). Policy-ranking metrics are themselves unstable on small or poorly separated policy sets ([arXiv 2606.15032](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15032)), and the Veo study reports no confidence intervals ([Emergent Mind summary](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2512.10675)).

Dexterous hands make all of this worse, and their evaluation has gone backwards. PDDM (2019) did MPC with an ensemble of dynamics models on a real 24-DoF hand. It named drops and contact switching as "sharp discontinuities in the dynamics" ([PDDM](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v100/nagabandi20a/nagabandi20a.pdf)). The 2025–26 hand world models trained on egocentric human video report FVD, FID and PCK. DexAC-WM, trained on EgoDex and EgoVerse-A, reports +6.18 PCK@10 on EgoDex and +3.0 on EgoVerse ([DexAC-WM](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.27325v1)). DexWM needs an auxiliary hand-consistency loss, and its robot transfer covers only grasp, reach and place ([DexWM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.13644)). None of them reports planning success on contact-rich tasks or accuracy on drop or slip events. Tactile world models are appearing, and DTWM finds that graded tactile location and magnitude beat binary contact for prediction ([DTWM](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.34286)). Most of that cluster was seen only at title level.

## Your hypothesis overlaps three lines of work, and needs one correction

The failure-data half of your hypothesis is already partly claimed. **"World Models Dream of Success"** fine-tunes released world models on execution-verified counterfactual replays. At equal failure count, counterfactual failures give a success–failure value gap of **0.124 versus 0.014** for on-policy failures. On 484 held-out LIBERO counterfactuals, optimism falls from **80% to 30–43%** ([arXiv 2610.09134](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09134)). Its stated limitation is that it needs executable counterfactual actions and verifiable outcomes. 1X makes the same qualitative point: without failure examples its world model is optimistically biased ([1X report](https://www.1x.tech/1x-world-model.pdf)). WMPO says the same about OXE-trained models ([WMPO](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.09515)), and WorldFailBench builds a benchmark around it ([arXiv 2610.33565](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.33565)). A reviewer will therefore read "failure data matters more than expressiveness" as known. Your differentiators are four. Those papers target **policy evaluation with parallel-jaw grippers on trained tasks**; you target **test-time planning on new objectives with a dexterous hand**. Your metric is **calibration against an explicit risk budget**, not success/failure discrimination. Your failures come from **task-agnostic play** rather than engineered counterfactual replays. And you localize the stochasticity to **contact transitions**. State this contrast in the first paragraph of the related work.

The spread-aware half overlaps with PDDM, whose ensemble existed precisely to represent uncertainty for dexterous MPC ([PDDM](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v100/nagabandi20a.html)), and with the risk-constrained MPPI line that uses explicit models ([BC-MPPI](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.00272)). The play-data angle overlaps with Play2Perfect. It finds that dexterous play pretraining transfers to precise assembly only when the play forces finger-level in-hand manipulation, and that loose play objectives do not transfer ([Play2Perfect](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26428v1)). Play content, not volume, is the variable. That predicts your result will depend on whether play visits the contact transitions your objectives stress.

Now the correction. Your own toy result does not show that expressiveness is unimportant. The mean-only model violated the 5% budget at 25%, while every spread-aware model landed at 4.8–7%. So **a minimal distributional output is necessary**, and failure coverage is necessary on top of it. What the toy suggests is that *beyond minimal stochasticity*, extra expressiveness buys little, while coverage of failures is what turns spread into calibrated risk. Phrase the hypothesis as two necessary conditions plus a saturation claim. Also note that 7% exceeds the 5% budget. Calibration is close but not achieved, and reviewers will notice. Finally, success-only models failing at 38–100% is close to true by construction: a model cannot assign probability to an event it has never seen. The non-obvious content has to come from dose-response (how much, which kind, where) and from real-hand evidence.

## Five questions a single RSS paper could credibly answer, ranked

The ranking weighs three things: how directly the question fills a gap the literature names, how much it differentiates you from Dream of Success, and whether a 5-page Stage-1 abstract with preliminary experiments can carry it. Questions 1–3 form one coherent paper. Question 4 is a minimal sanity arm. Question 5 is a stretch goal for Stage 2.

### 1. Is calibrated risk at contact transitions limited by data coverage or by model expressiveness?

Why the community cares: optimism and failure insensitivity are the most-cited world-model limitations ([Dream of Success](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09134); [MiraBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.29360)). Meanwhile, no study measures decision quality as a function of the fraction of failures in training, and no study isolates stochastic from deterministic predictors ([arXiv 2609.16074](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.16074v1)). What is known: counterfactual failures beat on-policy failures roughly 9× on value gap at equal count, but for evaluation, with grippers. The experiment is a factorial. Data arms are success-only, natural play including its own failures, and play plus targeted perturbations near contact, all at a matched number of transition events, plus a sweep of the failure fraction. Model arms are deterministic, heteroscedastic Gaussian, ensemble (the PDDM baseline) and diffusion/flow. The primary metric is **realized violation rate versus risk budget** across several budgets, plus a reliability diagram of predicted versus empirical drop probability, conditioned on contact phase. The headline figure is a 2D heatmap of violation-minus-budget over data × model. Your hypothesis predicts a sharp row effect (data) and a weak column effect once you leave the mean-only column. If diffusion clearly beats the ensemble at matched data, the "expressiveness matters less" claim is wrong and should be dropped. Comparing natural play failures with targeted perturbations is where you either confirm or extend Dream of Success's finding into dexterous, play-collected data.

### 2. Where is a hand's action-conditioned future actually multimodal?

Why the community cares: the mode-averaging argument for generative world models is mostly theoretical ([Aman primer](https://aman.ai/primers/world-modeling/)). Surveys flag "multimodal future prediction vs accurate action decoding" as an unresolved design tension ([arXiv 2609.16074](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.16074v1)). No controlled benchmark compares stochastic and deterministic world models on genuinely multimodal manipulation outcomes ([arXiv 2605.06388](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.06388)). What is known: PDDM names contact switching and drops as discontinuities ([PDDM](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v100/nagabandi20a/nagabandi20a.pdf)), but no recent paper measures them. The experiment: sample K futures per (state, action) from the most expressive model, and plot outcome dispersion (object-pose variance, a mode count via mixture-model selection) against time-to-contact-event. Then test whether a **deterministic predictor plus a learned transition-hazard head** matches the full generative model in planning. If it does, that is a design claim reviewers can reuse. One caution should change the hypothesis. Dispersion has three sources: true aleatoric contact bifurcation, unobserved object properties (mass, friction), and occlusion of the contact by the hand itself ([EgoDex limitations, secondary](https://liner.com/en/review/egodex-learning-dexterous-manipulation-from-largescale-egocentric-video)). Use the ensemble-vs-sample split to separate epistemic from aleatoric spread. Report whether multimodality also appears away from contacts when object properties vary. If it does, "nearly unimodal except at contact" should become "nearly unimodal given known object properties".

### 3. What limits planning toward objectives the model never trained on?

Why the community cares: no work was found that tests a genuinely new objective class (constraints, avoidance, risk) zero-shot on a learned latent or video world model for manipulation. Goal-image distance dominates partly because latent spaces like DINO's do not make arbitrary costs expressible ([V-JEPA 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.09985); [HWM](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.03208v2)). WAM "flexibility" claims, such as one model serving as policy, forward and inverse dynamics, are about inference modes, not new costs ([UWM](https://weirdlabuw.github.io/uwm/); [UVA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.00200)). Exploitation is asserted, not quantified ([arXiv 2606.00113](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00113)). The experiment attributes each failure on your three objectives (avoidance, centroid-to-location, risk-bounded carry) to one of three causes. First, **expressibility**: probe error when reading the objective-relevant quantity (centroid, obstacle distance, grasp margin) out of the learned state. Second, **model error** on that quantity over the planning horizon. Third, **exploitation**: the gap between predicted and realized cost as planner samples grow, the optimizer's-curse curve the literature lacks. Compare an object-centric or state-readout latent against a generic visual latent. This makes the "can the learned object express the objective" half of your hypothesis measurable instead of rhetorical. It also directly answers "Beyond Visual Quality", which found that selectors without a decision-relevant signal recover little oracle headroom ([arXiv 2609.24745](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24745)). The prediction worth stating up front: risk-bounded carry fails through calibration (Question 1), and avoidance and centroid fail through expressibility or exploitation.

### 4. On the same play data, does world-model planning beat a goal-conditioned policy or a WAM on new objectives?

Why the community cares: the most repeated gap is the absence of any same-data, same-compute comparison of policy, world model plus planning, and joint WAM ([arXiv 2603.22078](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22078); [arXiv 2609.24048](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.24048)). The π0.5 counterexample makes "data diversity alone" the null hypothesis to beat ([arXiv 2603.22078](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22078)). For an abstract, run the minimal version. Train a hindsight-relabelled goal-conditioned diffusion policy on the play data and steer it with cost guidance. Compare it with your world model plus sampling MPC and with a UWM-style joint model ([UWM](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.02792v3)) planned the same way. Use one in-distribution goal-reaching task as a control and the three new objectives as the test. The expected and useful finding is that policies match on goal reaching but cannot honour risk budgets they were never conditioned on. That makes the case for an explicit model at test time, which the "dream less, act more" trend otherwise undercuts ([survey 2606.20781](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.20781)). If the WAM matches your world model, report it. Your thesis is about data and calibration, not architecture, so a tie strengthens it.

### 5. Does egocentric human hand data improve or degrade risk calibration?

Why the community cares: human data plus a little robot data reliably helps policies ([EgoMimic](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.24221v1); [HAT](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.13441)). Zero-robot transfer breaks down on precise, contact-rich tasks because retargeted actions are geometrically imprecise ([H2RBench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.24778)). No study compares human video as world-model data versus policy data, or measures the value of human versus robot hours inside a world model. Human demonstrations are almost all successes, so they should inherit the optimism bias. The experiment: pretrain on EgoVerse, fine-tune on robot play, and measure Question 1's calibration metrics against no human pretraining. The likely result is that nominal free-space dynamics improve while contact-transition calibration improves only if robot play supplies the failures. That would sharpen your hypothesis: human data buys expressibility (Question 3), and failures buy calibration (Question 1). Rank it last for the abstract, because the embodiment gap adds a confound five pages cannot resolve. Keep it as the Stage-2 extension that uses your unique data asset, and cite DexAC-WM's EgoVerse results as the fidelity-only baseline you go beyond ([DexAC-WM](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.27325v1)).

## Missing evaluations that will make reviewers trust the result

The field has told you which evidence it discounts. Report decision-level metrics (violation versus budget, objective cost, success) above any FVD or PSNR, and do not merge levels into one score ([arXiv 2606.15032](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15032)). Use interventional tests: the same state under different actions, especially off-nominal ones near contact, since that is where models are known to ignore actions ([WorldEcho](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.24885v1)). Report bootstrap confidence intervals on violation rates. A 5% budget needs hundreds of carries per cell to tell 5% from 7%, and small-sample statistics are a known weakness of this literature ([Emergent Mind on Veo eval](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2512.10675)). Include the predicted-versus-realized cost curve over planning budget, because no one has published one for manipulation. Stratify every number by contact phase. No existing world-model correlation or accuracy result is split by contact-rich versus free-space segments, and that split is the empirical core of your hypothesis.

## Conclusion

The leverage in this paper comes from changing the unit of evaluation, not from adding model variants. The literature has accepted that success-only world models are optimistic, but it measures this as a ranking or discrimination problem on trained tasks. Recasting it as **calibration of risk at contact transitions, under explicit budgets, on objectives the model never saw** turns a known diagnosis into a usable design rule. That rule says represent spread minimally, concentrate it where contacts change, and spend data on failures near those transitions. It also answers the "million things to test" worry. The factorial in Question 1 and the attribution in Question 3 decide most of what matters. Everything else, including architecture choice between world model and WAM, becomes a control condition.

The riskiest assumption is not the failure-data claim, which is likely true. It is that hand futures are unimodal away from contact. If unobserved object properties or self-occlusion spread predictions during free-space carrying, the hazard-head simplification fails and expressiveness matters more than your toy suggests. Test that first, since it is cheap and decides which version of the hypothesis goes in the abstract. The caveat applies again: these findings come from search summaries rather than full texts, so verify every number and every 2026 arXiv ID at source before submission.
