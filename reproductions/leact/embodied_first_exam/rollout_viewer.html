Training versus evaluation: what is shared, and what differs

The retained training corpus contains 256 expert decision states from MetaWorld+, RoboCasa and Embodied Safety Exam. The development evaluations use held-out tasks from those suites plus DexHand. No DexHand state survived into this training corpus.

Training predicts expert actions from recorded expert histories. Evaluation runs Qwen freely, with its own actions and resulting tool feedback. The seven task IDs shown in the rollout panel are evaluation tasks; the training examples are different task instances.

Exact model-input parity is unresolved: an audited image-processing probe produced 3,334 image tokens in training and 4,018 through legacy native serving. Training uses complete examples within a 32K context budget; native evaluation uses its own longer-history compaction. The current warmstart and LeAct evaluations preserve the same native serving condition for comparison.

The matching initial-state hashes below compare Astra and Qwen on each development task. They do not mean that the development state was used for training. These results cannot isolate generalization from the remaining training/serving differences.

Each checkpoint has its own saved rollout. Switching changes the video, actions and grader receipt together.

How does Qwen behave on the training tasks?

These are the successful teacher episodes that supplied the retained warmstart corpus. Each full video includes actions beyond the retained examples. The marked decisions identify the exact actions kept for training.

LeAct generated explanations on a subset of these fixed expert states. “Drawn in LeAct” means the 12-update schedule actually used a new LeAct example from that state; “warmstart replay” means it reused the original teacher example. The Astra recordings document the original demonstrations. The student panel reports new closed-loop Qwen rollouts on these same task instances, without expert actions supplied.

Replay time follows the simulator; trace times follow agent wall time. Selecting a retained decision highlights its recorded tool call without claiming an exact video timestamp.

How LeAct chose the student explanations

The expert action was already given when these backward explanations were generated. This panel shows training data; the videos above show separate development evaluations.

Selection uses the gain in mean log-likelihood of the same complete expert action, in nats per token. Positive gain is an admission criterion; task success comes from the simulator grader.

Scope, provenance and how to read the comparison

The seven comparison tasks are in the development partition. The Training episodes tab contains the separate teacher recordings that supplied the warmstart corpus; their successful outcomes reflect selection into that corpus, not an unbiased training-task score. This is a small diagnostic screen, not a score on the full EFE benchmark or its core holdout. The five teacher successes include four manipulation tasks and one correctly handled safety task. Both teacher failures are also included. For every available checkpoint, the initial simulator-state SHA-256 matches its teacher episode. A pending LeAct task has no replacement or fabricated video.

“Model requests” counts inference requests. “Simulator calls” counts calls to the simulator API, including observations. “Control steps” comes from the grader’s suite-specific counter and measures commanded robot steps; these are different quantities.

Rollout traces contain recorded shell/code actions, returned feedback and public assistant messages. Brief behavior descriptions summarize those observations. The reasoning panel contains explicit backward explanations from the training corpus and generated student candidates. Provider-private reasoning and agent analysis messages are not exported.

Teacher and student use the same task IDs, but their model serving and context-compaction paths differ. This viewer supports behavioral inspection, not a matched latency or speedup claim. Development results were inspected during iteration.
